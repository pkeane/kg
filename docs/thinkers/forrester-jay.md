# Jay Forrester

American electrical engineer and founder of system dynamics, the modeling method that treats social and economic systems as networks of stocks, flows, feedback loops, and delays whose behavior over time can be simulated. He came to the social sciences from control engineering and computing, and the transfer of servomechanism concepts — feedback, gain, lag — into organizational and economic analysis is his central methodological move.

Forrester was born on a cattle ranch near Anselmo, Nebraska, took a degree in electrical engineering at the University of Nebraska in 1939, and joined MIT's Servomechanisms Laboratory. He directed Project Whirlwind, one of the first real-time digital computers, and in the late 1940s invented coincident-current random-access magnetic-core memory, which remained the dominant computer memory technology into the 1970s; he also led computer work for the SAGE air-defense system. He moved to the MIT Sloan School of Management in 1956, where he spent the rest of his career.

*Industrial Dynamics* (1961) applied feedback modeling to production and distribution, including the amplification of demand variability along supply chains later known as the bullwhip or Forrester effect; the MIT "Beer Game" teaching exercise derives from it. *Urban Dynamics* (1969) modeled a city as an interacting system of housing, business, and population stocks and concluded that low-income housing construction would deepen urban decline — a result that drew sustained criticism from urban planners and social scientists. His general claim, stated in "Counterintuitive Behavior of Social Systems" (1971), is that complex systems respond to intervention in ways that contradict intuition, so policies aimed at symptoms often make matters worse.

*World Dynamics* (1971) presented World2, a global model of population, capital, resources, food, and pollution built after Forrester's contact with Aurelio Peccei and the Club of Rome. [[meadows-donella|Donella Meadows]], Dennis Meadows, and their MIT team extended it into World3, the basis of *The Limits to Growth* (1972). Forrester spent his later decades on the National Model of the US economy and on the System Dynamics in Education Project, which sought to introduce the method into secondary schooling.

## Key ideas

- *System dynamics* — simulation of social systems as stock-and-flow structures with feedback and delay
- *Counterintuitive behavior of social systems* — the mismatch between intuitive policy and actual system response
- *Structure determines behavior* — persistent patterns are properties of a system's loop structure, not of external events
- *Bullwhip (Forrester) effect* — demand variability amplified upstream through a distribution chain

Isabella Weber

1987 – ? · German
#economics#political-economy#china#inflation

German economist, born in Nuremberg in 1987, on the economics faculty of the University of Massachusetts Amherst since 2019, where she is also research leader for China at the Political Economy Research Institute (PERI). She studied at the Free University of Berlin and Peking University and holds two doctorates: a PhD in Development Studies from the University of Cambridge (2018, supervised by Peter Nolan) and a PhD in Economics from the New School for Social Research (2019). She taught at Goldsmiths, University of London, from 2017 to 2019.

Her Cambridge thesis became How China Escaped Shock Therapy: The Market Reform Debate (2021). The book reconstructs the Chinese debate of the 1980s over price reform. One camp, influenced by Eastern European reformers and visiting Western economists, proposed a "big bang" liberalization of all prices at once. The other favored a dual-track system in which state-set prices for planned quotas coexisted with market prices at the margin, so that markets were introduced gradually from the edges of the planned economy. Weber argues that the leadership's rejection of the big-bang option in 1986 and again in 1988 is a main reason China's reform path differed from Russia's in the 1990s. She traces the Chinese reformers' approach to price stabilization back to older Chinese statecraft, including the Guanzi and the ever-normal granary tradition, and to the Communist Party's handling of hyperinflation in the late 1940s.

In December 2021 Weber published a Guardian op-ed proposing strategic price controls as a response to post-pandemic inflation. Paul Krugman called the idea "truly stupid" on Twitter, then deleted the post and apologized. Her subsequent work with Evan Wasner, "Sellers' Inflation, Profits and Conflict" (Review of Keynesian Economics, 2023), argues that firms in concentrated sectors used supply shocks as an occasion for coordinated price increases, so that profits drove part of the inflation. With collaborators she has also developed the idea of "systemically significant prices," identifying through input-output analysis the small set of sectors (energy, food, basic inputs) whose price movements propagate through the whole economy. In 2022 she and Sebastian Dullien proposed a gas price cap for German households, a version of which the German government later adopted. The work draws on John Kenneth Galbraith's writing on price control during the Second World War and on Karl Polanyi.

Key ideas

  • Dual-track price reform as an alternative to shock therapy
  • Price stabilization as a long-standing function of the Chinese state
  • Sellers' inflation: profit-led price increases in concentrated sectors during supply shocks
  • Systemically significant prices and targeted price stabilization
  • Strategic price controls as an emergency tool

Key works
